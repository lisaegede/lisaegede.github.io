Hi, my name is Lisa!

Product Development and Design | Houston, TX

Chevron: Building a tool employees could depend on

When I joined Chevron, the IT department was in the middle of a move to cloud tools. Longtime employees were adjusting to new systems, and the application they used to track performance metrics had some frustrating problems: comments disappeared, records had no timestamps, and there wasn’t a clear way to tell who had changed what. My summer project was to build a better way to manage those records using Microsoft PowerApps.

As the sole developer on the application, I worked with full-time Chevron employees across four Agile sprints, gathering feedback as I built and bringing it into the next round of changes.

Ambrosia: Making it easier to get to the game

If you've ever used emulators, you know that getting to the actual game can sometimes involve navigating multiple launchers, folders, and settings. A few friends and I wanted to make that process easier.

We started Ambrosia as a way to organize and launch games from one place. Before jumping into development, we surveyed gamers in computer science classes and Discord communities to understand how they managed their game libraries and what frustrated them.

We used that feedback to prioritize features, map out user flows, and design the interface together. My work focused on the research and front-end experience, including translating early ideas into wireframes and interactive mockups.

Before writing code, we surveyed gamers across computer science courses and Discord communities to prioritize features.

University of Oklahoma: A campus resource students had a reason to use

Studying abroad in Italy came with small daily questions: Where’s the nearest pharmacy? How do I get groceries? Will a transit strike affect my weekend plans? Our campus had a touchscreen in the monastery lobby, but students wasn't using it. During my media internship, I worked with the campus director to turn it into an engaging, practical resource.

The touch kiosk was situated directly in the monastery lobby. I set up a dedicated UX workstation area to rapid-prototype content updates during the semester.

Carnegie Mellon HCI Ph.D. Focus

The Questions Behind My AI Research

A lot of my work starts with a question: does this actually work for the person using it?

With AI, that question gets complicated. A response can sound convincing without being accurate or useful. People can interpret the same system differently depending on their experiences, expectations, and the situation they're using it in.

I've explored these questions from a few angles, including how AI practitioners evaluate fairness, how people from different communities experience AI assistants, and how design choices influence trust and reliance. Lately, I've been really interested in what these findings mean for the people building and evaluating AI products!

🏆 DIS 2024 — Honorable Mention

"For Us By Us": Intentionally Designing Technology for Lived Black Experiences

Lisa Egede, Leslie Coney, Brittany Johnson, Christina N. Harrington, Denae Ford • ACM DIS 2024

ACM DL

We interviewed designers and technologists creating products for Black communities to understand what they wanted their work to make possible beyond simply avoiding harm.

Core Contribution: Translates qualitative findings into explicit co-design frameworks for AI tools, moving beyond standard inclusion to active community-led tech development.

CSCW 2022

Assessing the Fairness of AI Systems: AI Practitioners' Processes, Challenges, and Needs for Support

Michael Madaio, Lisa Egede, Hariharan Subramonyam, Jenn Wortman Vaughan, Hanna Wallach • ACM CSCW 2022

ACM DL

We talked with AI practitioners about how they evaluate fairness in practice, where existing tools fall short, and what would make those tools more useful.

Methodology: Qualitative analysis of 33 practitioners across major technology enterprises, uncovering workflow bottlenecks in AI evaluation tools.
